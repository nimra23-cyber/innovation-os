# Actionable Recommendations Standard

## Purpose
Every recommendation produced by InnovationOS must be specific, actionable, and ranked by impact. Vague or generic advice provides no value.

## Required Structure

Every recommendation MUST include all three of the following elements:

### 1. Action Verb
Start the recommendation with a specific action verb. Approved verbs include:
- **Conduct** (research, interviews, surveys, tests)
- **Build** (prototypes, MVPs, landing pages, demos)
- **Partner** (with universities, organizations, companies, communities)
- **Validate** (assumptions, demand, pricing, technical approach)
- **Define** (target segment, value proposition, success metrics)
- **Launch** (beta program, pilot, waitlist, social campaign)
- **Analyze** (competitors, user feedback, market data)
- **Create** (content, documentation, pitch materials)
- **Apply** (to accelerators, grants, competitions)
- **Interview** (target users, domain experts, potential partners)

### 2. Target Outcome
After the action verb, clearly state what the recommendation aims to achieve. The target outcome must be specific and meaningful to the startup's success.

### 3. Next Step
End every recommendation with a concrete first step the founder can take within the next 7 days.

## Ranking
- All recommendation lists MUST be ranked by estimated impact from highest (rank 1) to lowest.
- Impact is assessed as: potential to significantly reduce the highest-priority risk OR to most directly advance the startup toward its stated goals.
- Do not present recommendations in random order.

## Examples

**Good recommendation:**
"Conduct 15 structured user interviews with your target audience (university students aged 18–25 who have expressed interest in entrepreneurship) to validate that the problem statement resonates and that your proposed solution addresses their primary pain point. Next step: Create a 10-question interview guide this week and reach out to 5 students in your network to schedule calls."

**Bad recommendation:**
"Consider doing some market research to understand your customers better."
