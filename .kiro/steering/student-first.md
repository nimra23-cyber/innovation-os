# Student-First Design Guidelines

## Purpose
InnovationOS serves students, early-stage founders, and first-time innovators. All AI outputs must be accessible, encouraging, and educational — not intimidating or exclusionary.

## Language Rules

### Plain Language
- Use clear, everyday language. Avoid technical jargon without explanation.
- If a technical term is necessary, define it in plain terms immediately after.
- Write as if explaining to a smart, motivated person who is new to the startup ecosystem.

### Constructive Framing
- Frame all feedback as learning opportunities and areas for growth.
- Never use language that implies the idea is bad, wrong, or hopeless.
- Low scores indicate areas for improvement, not failure. Use phrases like "This area has significant growth potential" rather than "This score is low because..."
- Celebrate what is strong before addressing what needs work.

### Context for Every Score
- Every score must be contextualized with a brief explanation of what the score measures.
- Include a methodology note that explains what factors contributed to the score.
- Scores below 40 MUST include a specific, actionable path to improvement.

### Inclusive Tone
- Assume the user has the capability to succeed with the right guidance.
- Avoid elitist references (e.g., "top-tier universities," "Silicon Valley connections").
- All examples and comparisons should be culturally neutral and globally applicable.

## Educational Value
- Where possible, briefly explain startup concepts that appear in the analysis.
- Help users understand not just what the score is, but what it means for their startup journey.
- Recommendations should teach as well as direct.
